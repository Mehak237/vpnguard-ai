FEASIBILITY // SLIDE 4 OF 6
 Feasibility Analysis & Risk Mitigation
High Feasibility

Technical Feasibility Analysis

  • Pure Software Implementation: Requires zero specialized hardware or proprietary appliances; runs on standard servers or SOC analyst laptops.
  • Sub-2-Second Audit Execution: Dissection and ML inference complete in under 2 seconds for a standard 1,000-packet capture.
  • Non-Intrusive & Passive: Listens passively to network taps/mirrors or parses exported PCAPs without emitting noisy probes that trigger IDS alarms.
  • Proven Foundation: Built on industry-standard RFC 7296 (IKEv2), RFC 4303 (ESP), and Scapy protocol parsers.
Identified Risks

Potential Challenges & Edge Cases

  • Traffic Jitter & Noise: High packet burstiness or non-standard MTUs might obscure padding ratios in low packet volume captures (<10 packets).
  • Proprietary / Vendor Extensions: Non-standard vendor transforms (e.g., custom Cisco/Fortinet payload extensions).
  • Zero-Day Cipher Implementations: Newly introduced Post-Quantum hybrid algorithms without standard IANA transform IDs.
Mitigation Strategies

Engineered Solutions

  • Ensemble Confidence Thresholds: The ML classifier outputs confidence scores and triggers an "Insufficient Packets" fallback alert if sample size is under threshold.
  • Hybrid Dissection Engine: Combines Scapy layer decoding with raw byte-level regex fallback to capture proprietary vendor transforms.
  • Modular Rule System: NIST SP 800-77r1 and CNSA 2.0 policies are decoupled into JSON/Python configuration matrices, allowing instant updates as new RFCs emerge.

RESEARCH AND REFERENCES

Standards, Cryptographic RFCs & Scholarly Citations

REFERENCES // SLIDE 6 OF 6
 Standards, Literature & Reference Work
Government & Defense Standards

Official Cybersecurity Standards

  • NIST SP 800-77 Rev. 1: "Guide to IPsec VPNs" — Outlines baseline requirements for IKEv2, approved cryptographic algorithms, and forward secrecy.
  • NSA Commercial National Security Algorithm (CNSA 2.0): Mandates AES-256-GCM, SHA-384, and post-quantum readiness for national security systems.
  • NIST SP 800-131A Rev. 2: "Transitioning the Use of Cryptographic Algorithms and Key Lengths" — Formal deprecation of 1024-bit Diffie-Hellman (Group 2) and SHA-1.
  • IETF RFC 7296: Internet Key Exchange Protocol Version 2 (IKEv2) standard specification.
  • IETF RFC 4303: IP Encapsulating Security Payload (ESP) protocol and padding specification.
Academic Research & CVEs

Cryptanalytic Literature & ML Research

  • CVE-2016-2183 (Sweet32): Bhargavan & Leurent, "On the Practical (In-)Security of 64-bit Block Ciphers in TLS and IPsec" (ACM CCS 2016). Proves collision attacks on 3DES.
  • CVE-2015-4000 (Logjam): Adrian et al., "Imperfect Forward Secrecy: How Diffie-Hellman Fails in Practice" (ACM CCS 2015). Proves state precomputation against 1024-bit MODP.
  • Encrypted Traffic Classification: Wang et al., "Machine Learning for Encrypted Traffic Analysis: A Survey" (IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials, 2022).
  • strongSwan Open-Source IPsec Project: Tested against strongSwan v5.9 containerized lab testbed.
